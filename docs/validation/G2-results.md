# Gate G2 results: LSU PERTT large-diameter air-water runs

Generated by `wellsim/validation/g2_lsu.py` from
`docs/validation/data/lsu_table_a1.csv` (LSU Final Report Table A.1, PDF pp.
155-158). Point values, not a march: the reports give no tap spacing and no
per-run pressure, so there is nothing to integrate over. At each row's stated
superficial velocities the gate builds a one-segment pipe and calls the march's
own `two_phase_gradient`; the number scored is that function's `dp_dx`.

131 runs; 93 report a liquid holdup.

## g2-4: the gate now runs the march's gradient

Until 23 Sep 2026 the gate carried **its own** gradient. It used the homogeneous
acceleration term

    dp/dz = (grav + fric) / (1 - E_k),   E_k = rho_m v_m v_sg / (g_c p 144)

which M3-2b had already superseded in the march. The march differentiates the
SLIP momentum flux G_g v_g + G_l v_l, so each phase accelerates at its own
velocity through the closure's `dalpha/drho_g`, and the singular point is det = 0
of the 2x2 momentum-energy system rather than E_k = 1. That reimplementation is
deleted. The gate now duplicates no core formula and cannot drift from the thing
it grades.

The difference is not cosmetic, because E_k goes as 1/p and this campaign is at
14.7 psia. Every gradient number in this document moved:

**Which errors were artifacts of the gate's own gradient, and which survive.**

- The **churn and churn/annular** errors were substantially the gate's. Churn
  nearly halves and churn/annular falls by a third. Those rows sit at high gas
  rate where the homogeneous E_k is largest, and the homogeneous form was
  inflating the prediction there.
- The **annular** error is entirely the closure's. 61.07% to
  61.08% on the same two rows: the acceleration term was never
  the problem there. Everything G2-2 concluded about annular stands.
- The **0.10 m and 0.30 m blocks** improve modestly; the **0.20 m** block nearly
  halves, for the same reason as churn (it holds most of the high-rate rows).
- The **low-slip pass criterion gets slightly worse**, 2.97% to
  3.84%, and still passes its 10% bar
  with room. Those 14 rows are slow and liquid-dominated, so the acceleration
  term is small in either form; the move is not evidence about either gradient.

No row is non-evaluable under either form: the old E_k never reached 1 (its
largest value is 0.548, on run 40) and det never reaches 0.

## The holdup column is screened; the gradient column is not

A data consistency screen runs before any scoring
(`docs/validation/G2-consistency.md`). Stripping friction and acceleration from
the reported TOTAL gradient leaves hydrostatic, which fixes a liquid holdup. On
35 of the 93 rows that report a holdup, that implied value differs
from the reported one by more than the run's own stated measurement uncertainty.
The two reported columns cannot both be true there, and the model is not graded
against a number its own source contradicts.

So: **gradient is scored on all 131 rows; holdup only on the 58 that
pass the screen.** Nothing is tuned to either column.

g2-4 moved this count from 54 to 58 (49 runs are kept by both). The
screen subtracts an acceleration term, and that term is now the march's
`dp/dx x (1 - det)` rather than the homogeneous one, so it is smaller at high gas
rate and the screen is correspondingly less aggressive. The screen's own logic is
untouched.

Read the screened holdup numbers per diameter, not overall. Screening changes the
MIX: the 3.83 in section has a +/-0.19 holdup tolerance so it keeps most of its
rows, while the tighter sections shed most of theirs, and that section is also
where the closure does worst. Per diameter every number improves; the overall
number rises only because the survivors are now dominated by the hardest section.

## What this gate can and cannot validate

**The gradient column is the gate's basis.** Nothing impugns it: it is internally
consistent, it varies smoothly within every liquid-rate block, and wherever the
two reported columns disagree it is the holdup that is implausible. All 131 rows
are scored on it.

**Holdup above about 15 ft/s of mixture velocity cannot be validated against this
dataset at all.** Not "is validated poorly" - cannot be. Two reasons, both in
docs/validation/G2-consistency.md:

1. The sign flip. Above 15 ft/s the holdup implied by the reported gradient
   departs from the reported holdup by +0.059 in the 3.83 in section and by
   -0.127 and -0.113 in the 7.83 and 11.7 in sections. It is not that the data
   are noisy: they are biased, and biased in OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS in different
   pipes. No single physical effect does that, so at least two of the three
   sections must be wrong at high rate, and the dataset cannot say which. Below
   5 ft/s the same comparison gives -0.008, +0.001 and -0.030, so the low-rate
   holdups are usable.

2. Row 1 shows the failure is real and not an artefact of the screen. Its
   reported holdup of 0.99 needs the gas to move in situ at 23 times the
   drift-flux velocity - 43.96 ft/s in a bubbly flow whose entire mixture
   velocity is 0.90 ft/s. That check uses neither the gradient column nor any
   pressure assumption. A column that can contain that value cannot be used to
   grade a closure to a few per cent.

So: the screened holdup numbers below are a CROSS-CHECK, never a fit target. No
coefficient in this project is tuned to them, and none should be.

## Read this before using the gate to justify anything

### The gate's worst regime is the tool's main use case

This is not a gate that validates the tool where it is used. It validates it
where it is easy and fails where it matters.

    gradient MAE   bubble 3.99%   bubbly 5.39%   slug/churn 4.36%
                   churn 18.17%   annular 61.08%   churn/annular 54.35%

A blowout is annular essentially everywhere. The shipped `gas_water_wgr` example
is annular at **all 125 march points**, with alpha_g 0.9751 at the sandface and
**0.9986 at the choked exit**. So every number the tool produces for its actual
purpose comes out of the regime where this gate measures 61% error on
gradient, and none of it comes from the bubbly and slug regimes where the gate
measures 4 to 5%. Quoting the overall 14.42% as though it applied to a
blowout calculation would be wrong.

g2-4 sharpens this rather than softening it. The overall figure improved by a
third, but the annular figure did not move at all, so the gap between where the
gate is good and where the tool is used is now WIDER, not narrower.

### These annular errors BOUND the downhole error, they do not estimate it

LSU ran air and water at about 14.7 psia. The liquid-to-gas density ratio there
is about **832:1**. Downhole it is nothing like that:

    LSU air-water, 14.7 psia, 70 degF        rho_l/rho_g   832
    gas_water_wgr at the exit, 157.9 psia    rho_l/rho_g   103
    gas_water_wgr at the sandface, 3,688 psia rho_l/rho_g    5.6

A factor of 150 in density ratio at the sandface. Slip, the drift velocity and
the distribution parameter all depend on that ratio, and every one of them is
hardest at the extreme LSU occupies: a near-weightless gas over a heavy film is
the most slip-dominated case there is. At 5.6:1 the phases are close to
homogeneous and the closure has far less work to do.

So the right reading of the annular numbers is as an UPPER BOUND on the downhole
error, not an estimate of it. They say the closure is not worse than this in
annular flow; they do not say it is this bad at 3,688 psia. Nothing in this
dataset can say what the downhole annular error actually is, because no
large-diameter annular data at a downhole density ratio has been read.

### The 0.20 m block: concentrated, and now unambiguously friction

The 0.20 m section is the worst of the three at 25.04%, and the error is
concentrated rather than spread. 9 of its 28 rows carry 69.9% of the block error,
every one of them over-predicting, and every one above 19.8 ft/s of mixture
velocity. The closure predicts annular on 0 of the 28.

g2-4 settles what the over-prediction is. On those 9 rows the march's
acceleration term is 0.0002 to 0.002 psi/ft against measured gradients of 0.057
to 0.109 - between 0.3% and 2% of the total. Acceleration is therefore ruled out
entirely, which the homogeneous form could not do. What is left is gravity plus
friction, and on 7 of the 9 the measured TOTAL is at or below the hydrostatic
gradient computed at the row's own REPORTED holdup:

    run    H_l pred   H_l rep   grav pred   grav at rep   measured
    ------------------------------------------------------------------
     48      0.1713      0.13     0.07454       0.05624    0.05685
     49      0.1530      0.12     0.06663       0.05192    0.05734
     45      0.2412      0.17     0.10475       0.07355    0.07568
     59      0.1876      0.13     0.08158       0.05624    0.08090
     46      0.1855      0.20     0.08067       0.08653    0.07391
     47      0.1834      0.21     0.07977       0.09085    0.08351
     58      0.1969      0.15     0.08563       0.06490    0.09315
     68      0.2036      0.26     0.08849       0.11249    0.10897
     67      0.2087      0.22     0.09068       0.09518    0.10844

Run 48 is the cleanest: gravity at the reported holdup is 0.05624 against a
measured total of 0.05685, 99% of it, leaving essentially no room for a friction
term - and the model adds 0.04004. Averaged over the 9, gravity alone at the
reported holdup is 94% of the measured total. Two rows (46, 47) over-predict in
total while UNDER-predicting holdup, which only friction can do.

So the 0.20 m block indicts the homogeneous mixture friction, `f rho_m v_m^2`,
not the closure. A gas core over a slow liquid film does not dissipate like a
single fluid at the mixture density and the mixture velocity.

The Beggs-Brill cross-check corroborates that, and it is the one place in this
document where Beggs-Brill beats the model path. Run through the same march
gradient it scores 9.85% on this block against the closure's
25.04%, and 9.60% in churn/annular against 54.35%, while
losing overall (18.46% against 14.42%) and badly in the 0.30 m block. Its
holdup is no better here. What differs is that Beggs-Brill carries its own
two-phase friction factor rather than a Moody factor at the mixture Reynolds
number. This is a pointer, not a proposal: the fix is a film model, not adopting
a correlation the project deliberately keeps off the model path.

    roughness     PVC 6e-5 in (4 and 12 in), steel 0.0018 in (8 in)

The water heat capacity and sound speed are new in g2-4: the march's gradient
solves a momentum-energy pair, so it needs them. Neither is a free parameter.
c_l enters only the Wallis mixture sound speed, which is a diagnostic and not in
dp/dx at all, and cp_l enters only the energy row, where it is dominated by the
liquid mass fraction.
